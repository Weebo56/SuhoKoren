About

I'm a backend engineer who spends most of my time in the layer between "the product works" and "the product works when 50,000 people hit it at once." That means queues, consensus, caching, and a healthy respect for what happens when a network call doesn't come back.

Most of my work sits at the seam between application code and infrastructure — designing APIs that are boring in the best way, and systems that fail loudly instead of silently. I care more about a service's worst day than its demo day.

Outside of client work, I maintain a couple of open-source libraries and spend an unreasonable amount of time reading postmortems for fun.
